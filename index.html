02

Major Research Findings

Across his research career, Karthikeyan has explored how tumors alter immune recognition, how the extracellular matrix contributes to immune escape, and how these mechanisms may influence the response to cancer immunotherapy.

DISCOVERY 01 · 2021

miR-21-3p, TGF-β and Tumor Immune Escape

This work identified a regulatory connection between miR-21-3p, TGF-β signaling, MHC class I expression and the extracellular-matrix protein biglycan in tumor cells.

Why it matters: The study connected microRNA regulation and extracellular-matrix biology with mechanisms controlling tumor immune recognition.

View Publication →
DISCOVERY 02 · 2023

PRELP and the Tumor Escape Phenotype

In melanoma, loss of PRELP expression was associated with features of an immune-escape phenotype, bringing attention to the role of the extracellular matrix in tumor immunogenicity.

Why it matters: The study helped establish PRELP as an important candidate linking extracellular-matrix remodeling with tumor immune escape.

View Publication →
DISCOVERY 03 · 2024

ACE2, Interferon Signaling and Anti-PD-1 Response

This study investigated alterations in ACE2 and interferon-related signaling and their relationship with tumor immunogenicity and response to anti-PD-1 therapy in solid tumors.

Why it matters: The work created an unusual connection between infection-associated molecular pathways and cancer immunotherapy.

View Publication →
DISCOVERY 04 · 2025

TMPRSS2 and Response to Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors

This research examined how TMPRSS2 expression relates to tumor immunogenicity and clinical response to immune checkpoint inhibitors across cancers.

Why it matters: It extended the investigation of viral-entry-associated molecules into the field of cancer immune biology and immunotherapy response.

View Publication →
